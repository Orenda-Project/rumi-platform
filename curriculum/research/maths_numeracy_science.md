## 0. The thesis in one paragraph

Primary maths is learned in a **fixed representational order** — first with things you can hold, then with pictures of those things, then with the bare symbols — and this order (Bruner's enactive → iconic → symbolic, operationalised by Singapore as **Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract**) is the single best-evidenced structural fact in the field. Our taxonomy is *already* built on it, and it is correct. But CPA is a **spine, not the whole animal**. Bolted onto that spine, the evidence demands three things a bare CPA sequence does not by itself guarantee: (1) an explicit, daily **number-sense** routine (subitising, part-whole/number bonds, place value, ten-frames) because early number sense is the strongest single predictor of later maths achievement; (2) deliberate, short, **retrieval-based fact-fluency practice** so that number facts become automatic and stop consuming working memory — the drilling debate is real, but it resolves cleanly into *do short, frequent, low-stakes retrieval; don't do long, high-stakes timed tests*; and (3) a **mastery** discipline — small steps, depth over coverage, **variation** in practice, and a **mastery check before moving on**. The verdict for our lesson-type design: **keep the five CPA types, but make three currently-implicit elements first-class and mandatory in every concept — a number-sense warm-up, a fact-fluency drill, and a mastery-check exit gate — and add one dedicated `fluency` element rather than a whole separate lesson type.** For our Urdu-L1 / English-medium children the same bilingual scaffolding the Science lane uses applies verbatim: English maths vocabulary is the term-of-record and the floor, Urdu is the meaning-negotiation scaffold, never a partial map.

# Part I — The research base

## 1. CPA / Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract — the spine (and it holds up)

**Origin.** CPA operationalises **Jerome Bruner's** three modes of representation (late 1960s): **enactive** (action on physical objects) → **iconic** (images/diagrams) → **symbolic** (language, numerals, notation). Learning is most durable when a new concept is met first enactively, then iconically, then symbolically. **Singapore** adopted this as the backbone of its primary maths from the 1980s and it is widely credited as a driver of Singapore's sustained top-of-table TIMSS/PISA performance. ([Structural Learning — CPA guide](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/concrete-pictorial-abstract-approaches-in-the-classroom) · [World Scientific — CPA in Singapore](https://www.worldscientific.com/doi/10.1142/9789811220159_0003) · [Third Space Learning — CPA](https://thirdspacelearning.com/blog/concrete-pictorial-abstract-maths-cpa/) · [White Rose Education — CPA](https://whiteroseeducation.com/latest-news/what-cpa-approach) · [EdShed — CPA explained](https://blog.edshed.com/what-is-the-cpa-approach-explained/))

**Why concrete-first (the mechanism).** A physical manipulation gives the child a referent the symbol can *point back to*. Without it, "carrying," "borrowing," or "×" become memorised finger-moves detached from quantity — which is precisely the origin of the most common misconceptions (Part 5). The concrete stage is where the *meaning* of the operation is built; the pictorial stage transfers that meaning to a portable representation (bar model, ten-frame, number line); the abstract stage is fluency with the notation once meaning is secure.

**Evidence of effect.** The strongest quantitative evidence is for **CRA** (Concrete-Representational-Abstract, the US name for CPA). A 2025 meta-analysis (Ebner, MacDonald, Grekov & Aspiranti, *Learning Disabilities Research & Practice*) of 30 single-case studies found a very large overall effect (Tau-BC ≈ 0.996) for CRA as a maths intervention, and — importantly for our design — found the **non-integrated** sequence (concrete, then representational, then abstract kept as distinct phases) *out-performed* integrated variants. ([Ebner et al. 2025, SAGE, DOI 10.1177/09388982241292299](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09388982241292299) · [ERIC EJ1469639](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1469639)). The UK **Education Endowment Foundation** makes "use **manipulatives and representations**" one of its headline recommendations for both Early Years/KS1 and KS2/3 maths — with the caveat that manipulatives must be used *purposefully*, with a clear rationale linking the specific manipulative to the specific concept, and then **deliberately faded** toward abstraction (a manipulative that never leaves the table becomes a crutch). ([EEF — Early Maths](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/early-maths) · [EEF — Maths KS2/3 PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED634883.pdf))

> **Caveat we must state honestly (Rule 12):** the Tau-BC ≈ 0.996 figure is from *single-case* designs on (often) learning-disabled samples — it is strong evidence that CRA *works as a structured sequence*, not a claim that every classroom sees a ~1.0 effect. The direction and robustness are what we rely on, not the decimal.

**Pressure-test of our taxonomy against CPA.** Our five types are `concrete → pictorial → pictorial_abstract → word_problem → revision`. Mapped to the research:
- `concrete` = Bruner **enactive** ✓
- `pictorial` = Bruner **iconic** ✓
- `pictorial_abstract` = the **bridge** from iconic to symbolic (pictures *with* numerals) ✓ — this is a genuinely useful intermediate the raw CPA triple often glosses over; keep it.
- `word_problem` = **application/transfer** — correctly separated, because reading a situation and choosing an operation is a distinct competency (and, for our EAL learners, a language-loaded one; Part 5).
- `revision` = **consolidation / spaced review** ✓ — but see §3: this is where spaced retrieval must live, not just "redo the exercises."

**The gap the CPA spine does NOT close on its own:** nothing in `concrete→pictorial→abstract` forces (a) a **number-sense** foundation before the sequence starts, (b) **fact-fluency automaticity**, or (c) a **mastery gate** that stops the class advancing over a shaky base. Those are Parts 2-4, and they are why the taxonomy needs the *elements* added in Part II even though the five *types* stay.

## 2. Number sense — the foundation, and the best predictor we have

**What it is.** "Number sense" = a flexible, intuitive feel for quantity and how numbers relate and come apart: **subitising** (instantly seeing "4" without counting), **part-whole** understanding (7 is 5-and-2 *and* 6-and-1 *and* 4-and-3), **number bonds**, **place value** (the base-10 structure), and fluent movement on a **number line**. Boaler's framing: low achievers are usually not low because they know less, but because they use numbers *inflexibly* — set early onto memorising procedures instead of interacting with quantity. ([YouCubed — Fluency Without Fear](https://www.youcubed.org/evidence/fluency-without-fear/) · [Calculate — Number Talks](https://calculate.org.au/2018/09/14/building-number-sense-through-number-talks/))

**Why it matters more than almost anything else.** Early number sense at school entry is one of the **strongest and most consistent predictors of later maths achievement**, with effects that persist through primary and into secondary school, and the predictive relationship replicates across England, Finland, Flanders, USA, Canada, and Taiwan. Children who start with weak number sense tend to *stay* low-achieving without intervention, and — critically — **early gaps widen over time** if unaddressed (the "Matthew effect" in numeracy). ([Predicting first-grade maths achievement, PMC3983481](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3983481/) · [Importance of number sense to achievement in grades 1 & 3, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608009000533) · [ANS precision predicts later maths, PMC3173357](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3173357/) · [CIS — Early Numbers, Big Ideas](https://www.cis.org.au/publication/early-numbers-big-ideas-fostering-number-sense-in-young-children/))

**Subitising specifically** is repeatedly identified as a predictor of future mathematical development; **conceptual subitising** (seeing 6 as two groups of 3) is a route into part-whole and early addition. ([Conceptual subitising & foundational number sense, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-23935-4_21))

**The tools that build it (all CPA-native):**
- **Ten-frame** — two rows of five; makes "making ten," place value, and part-whole visible. ([TeachableMath — Ten Frames & Number Bonds](https://teachablemath.com/ten-frames-number-bonds/))
- **Number bonds / part-part-whole** — the whole with its parts; the single model that makes addition and subtraction *the same relationship seen two ways*, which pre-empts the classic "reverts to adding when asked to subtract" error (Part 5). Part-whole is described as one of the most reliable predictors of whether a child develops genuine arithmetic fluency versus staying stuck on counting. ([Maths — No Problem! — Number Bonds](https://mathsnoproblem.com/en/approach/number-bonds) · [Math Coach's Corner — Number Bonds & Part/Whole](https://www.mathcoachscorner.com/2014/12/number-bonds-and-partwhole-thinking/))
- **Number line** and **base-10 (Dienes) blocks** for place value; **Cuisenaire rods** for relative magnitude. ([EEF — manipulatives](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3))
- **Number Talks** — 5-15 min daily mental-computation discussions where children share strategies. Boaler calls them "the best pedagogical method I know for developing number sense." ([Meaningful Math — Number Talks](https://www.meaningfulmathco.com/number-talks/) · [YouCubed](https://www.youcubed.org/evidence/fluency-without-fear/))

**Design consequence:** number sense is not a topic you "cover" in Term 1 and leave — it is a **daily warm-up routine** that runs *underneath* the CPA sequence all year. This is the first element we must make mandatory (Part II).

## 3. Fact fluency + drilling — the debate, and the decisive resolution

**The uncontested core.** Automaticity of basic number facts (addition/subtraction bonds, then times tables) matters because **retrieving a known fact from long-term memory frees working memory** for the actual problem. A child who computes 7×8 by repeated addition has no working memory left for the multi-step word problem the 7×8 sits inside. This is not controversial — it is basic cognitive load theory, and Boaler and the cognitive scientists **agree** that fluency is the goal. ([EdWeek — What is fact fluency](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/what-is-math-fact-fluency-and-how-does-it-develop/2023/05))

**The debate is about *method*, and it has two poles:**

**Pole A — "Fluency Without Fear" (Jo Boaler, Stanford/YouCubed).** Fluency should grow from **number sense and strategy**, not rote memorisation under time pressure. Boaler's specific, evidence-anchored objection is to **timed tests**: for about **one-third of students, timed testing is the onset of maths anxiety**, and — the mechanism — **stress blocks working memory**, so a child under time pressure literally cannot access facts they know. Students who learn facts through *strategy* transfer better to new problems than students who memorise blindly. ([Boaler — Fluency Without Fear (YouCubed)](https://www.youcubed.org/evidence/fluency-without-fear/) · [Boaler — Speed & time pressure blocks working memory (PDF)](https://www.dyslexicadvantage.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Speed_and_Time_Pressure_Blocks_Working_Memory_.pdf) · [Stanford Report](https://news.stanford.edu/2015/01/29/math-learning-boaler-012915/))

**Pole B — the cognitive-science / retrieval-practice camp (Agarwal, Fuchs, Rohrer).** **Retrieval practice** — actively recalling a fact rather than re-studying it — is one of the most robust findings in learning science, and it *builds* the long-term memory that automaticity requires. Pooja Agarwal's work shows **short, frequent** retrieval (e.g. flashcards) beats long, infrequent practice. Lynn Fuchs found children in **timed-practice** groups answered more facts correctly per day and committed more to long-term memory via **spaced retrieval**. ([Hechinger / Proof Points — do maths drills help?](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-do-math-drills-help-children-learn/) · [Ophuis-Cox 2023, *Applied Cognitive Psychology* — retrieval practice on multiplication facts](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.4141) · [Edutopia — should more time be spent on maths facts](https://www.edutopia.org/article/how-decreased-practice-time-plays-into-historic-math-declines/))

**These are not actually contradictory — and here is the resolution (decided):**
The two camps disagree on **timed, high-stakes testing**, not on **practice**. The synthesis both literatures support:

| Do this (evidence-backed, anxiety-safe) | Not this (anxiety-inducing, weaker) |
|---|---|
| **Short & frequent**: 2-10 min/day of retrieval | Long, occasional cram sessions |
| **Strategy-first, then automate**: build the fact via number sense (making-ten, doubles, "×8 is double ×4"), *then* drill for speed | Memorising a table with no meaning |
| **Low-stakes, self-referenced**: child races their *own* previous score; untimed or generous-time; mistakes are data | Whole-class timed tests, publicly ranked |
| **Spaced & interleaved**: mix fact types, revisit over days/weeks | Massed practice of one fact type once |
| **Retrieval format**: flashcards, quick oral quiz, games, "number of the day" | Passive re-reading of the times table |

**Dosage (concrete).** The classroom-research consensus for fact fluency is **~5-15 minutes daily**, and specialist programmes report automatic recall built in **weeks** with **2-5 minute daily** spaced-retrieval bursts. **Spacing** (revisit a fact type across days) and **interleaving** (juxtapose different fact types so the child must *choose* a strategy) both add durability; interleaving also improves problem-type discrimination. ([MathBuilders — 2-to-5-min daily method](https://mathbuilders.com/math-fact-fluency) · [Rohrer & Hartwig — Spaced & Interleaved Maths Practice (PDF)](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-spaced-and-interleaved-mathematics-practice-rohrer-hartwig.pdf) · [Interleaved practice improves maths learning, ERIC ED557355](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED557355.pdf) · [Australian Maths Hub — spaced/interleaved/retrieval](https://www.mathematicshub.edu.au/plan-teach-and-assess/teaching/teaching-strategies/spaced-interleaved-and-retrieval-practice/))

**The drilling verdict for us:** **Yes, drill — but drill the right way.** Every concept gets a short daily fact-fluency element that is (1) strategy-derived, (2) short and frequent, (3) low-stakes and self-referenced, (4) spaced and interleaved, and (5) game-framed to stay anxiety-safe. This is genuine drilling (retrieval under mild pace), not the "no memorisation" caricature and not the "Friday timed test" caricature. Both Boaler and Agarwal would sign the row above; they only fight over the right-hand column.

## 4. Mastery — small steps, depth over coverage, variation, and the check-before-you-move gate

**What "teaching for mastery" means (NCETM / Shanghai / Singapore).** Not "the top set races ahead" — the opposite: **the whole class moves together through a carefully constructed small-step journey**, and the class does not advance until the current step is secure. Depth is prioritised over coverage; the same concept is examined from multiple representations before the notation is drilled. The English mastery movement was explicitly reverse-engineered from Shanghai/Singapore after their PISA/TIMSS results. ([NCETM — is it just good teaching?](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/classroom-resources/cs-teaching-for-mastery-isn-t-it-just-good-teaching/) · [NCETM — supporting research & evidence](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/teaching-for-mastery/mastery-explained/supporting-research-evidence-and-argument/) · [Schools Week — where did maths mastery come from](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/where-did-maths-mastery-come-from/) · [Maths — No Problem! — mastery guide](https://mathsnoproblem.com/en/resources/maths-mastery-guide))

**Variation theory — the part most curricula miss.** The distinctive East-Asian lever is **variation**: (a) **conceptual variation** — show what the concept *is* and what it *is not*, across multiple representations; (b) **procedural variation** — in a practice set, change *one* thing at a time and hold the rest constant, so the child *notices the structure* ("intelligent practice") instead of grinding mechanical repetition. The routine prompt is **"What's the same? What's different?"** ([NCETM — how variation changed my teaching](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/features/how-variation-has-changed-my-teaching/) · [Ballantine — variation theory in practice](https://nicolaballantine.wordpress.com/2018/04/15/whats-the-same-whats-different-variation-theory-in-practice/))

**The mastery check.** Because gaps compound (Part 2), mastery teaching **checks for secure understanding before advancing** — a quick formative gate, not an end-of-unit test after it's too late to fix. This is the discipline our `revision` type currently under-specifies.

**Design consequence:** two elements become mandatory — **variation in the practice set** (not 20 near-identical sums) and a **mastery-check exit gate** on every concept's final CPA lesson.

## 5. Misconceptions, word problems, and bilingual maths language

**The high-frequency misconceptions to pre-empt** (each traceable to a *skipped concrete stage* or a *place-value gap*):

| Misconception | What the child does | Root cause | Pre-empt with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Place value / regrouping** | "Carries" and "borrows" as memorised digit-moves with no sense of quantity | Symbols introduced before base-10 quantity was made concrete | Dienes blocks + ten-frames *before* the algorithm ([Mathnasium — place value](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/paoli/news/place-value-mistakes-elementary-school)) |
| **Subtraction → reverts to adding** | Adds when the problem asks to subtract | Addition over-taught, subtraction under-taught, and taught as *separate* | The **part-part-whole** model, so +/− are one relationship ([Third Space — primary misconceptions](https://thirdspacelearning.com/blog/common-errors-misconceptions-primary-maths-ks1-ks2/)) |
| **"Bigger denominator = bigger fraction"** | Says 1/4 > 1/3 because 4 > 3 | Whole-number thinking applied to fractions | Concrete fraction bars/circles; "more parts = smaller parts" ([Maths — No Problem! — fractions misconceptions](https://mathsnoproblem.com/blog/teaching-tips/how-to-address-4-common-fractions-misconceptions)) |
| **Adds numerators AND denominators** | 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 | Transfers whole-number algorithm to fractions | Common-unit models first, notation last ([Student errors in fractions, ERIC EJ1161215](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1161215.pdf)) |
| **"=" means "the answer is"** | Can't handle 3 + □ = 7 | "=" taught only as "compute →" | Balance/number-bond framing of equality from G1 |

General NCETM misconception catalogue: [Misconceptions with the Key Objectives (NCETM DOC)](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/media/cocnx0it/misconceptions-with-the-key-objectives2.doc). The design move is the same each time: **build the concrete referent first; the misconception is what fills the vacuum when the abstract symbol arrives with no referent.**

**Word problems + mathematical language (and the bilingual layer).** Reading a situation and choosing the operation is a *distinct* competency — which is why `word_problem` is rightly its own type. But for **Urdu-L1 children doing maths in English**, the word problem is where the **language load**, not the maths, causes failure (the same BICS/CALP finding as the Science lane: academic language takes 5-7 years to develop even when conversational English sounds fluent). The bilingual maths-vocabulary layer is therefore mandatory:
- **Term of record = English** (اردو gloss alongside): *sum/جمع، difference/فرق، product/حاصلِ ضرب، quotient/خارجِ قسمت، numerator-denominator/شمار کنندہ-نام کنندہ، equals/برابر، place value/مقامی قدر، fraction/کسر، remainder/باقی*. English is the **floor** (it is the language of the exam and of secondary school), never the ceiling; never ship a partial map (Rule 20).
- **Translanguaging policy per type** mirrors the Science lane: Urdu encouraged for reasoning-aloud and for stating the *plan* of a word problem; the operation term and the written answer pinned in English.
- Every word problem must be checked for **hidden language traps** ("share equally" = ÷, "how many more" = −, "times as many" = ×) — these comparison/relational phrases are exactly where EAL learners misroute. (BICS/CALP + EMI-Pakistan evidence carried over from the Science research: [Colorín Colorado — BICS/CALP](https://www.colorincolorado.org/faq/what-are-bics-and-calp) · [English/Urdu-medium divide, ERIC EJ1216773](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1216773.pdf))

## 6. Grade-level benchmarks + the Pakistan numeracy crisis

**The crisis (why this is urgent, not academic).** ASER Pakistan 2023: nationally, the share of **Class 5** children who can do **two-digit division dropped from 51% (2021) to 46% (2023)** — i.e. **the majority of Grade 5 children cannot do a Grade-3-level division**, and it is getting *worse*. Regional floors are far lower (rural Sindh ~27%). Maths proficiency is described as "very low, and getting worse." ([Dawn — ASER dip in learning levels](https://www.dawn.com/news/1824238) · [Express Tribune — ASER primary numeracy](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2154226/pakistan-primary-students-struggle-literacy-numeracy-aser) · [PAL Network — ASER Pakistan](https://palnetwork.org/aser-pakistan/)). The ASER numeracy ladder itself (number recognition → 2-digit subtraction → 2-digit division) is the blunt national benchmark our LPs must actually move children up.

**End-of-grade proficiency targets** (synthesised from Common-Core-style progressions, adapted to a single-subject primary; [Savvy Learning — fluency benchmarks](https://savvylearning.com/blog/math-fluency-benchmarks-by-grade/) · [Rocket Math — benchmarks](https://www.rocketmath.com/2021/02/22/math-benchmarks-how-to-help-your-students-meet-them/)):

| End of | Number range | Fluent recall (automaticity) | New operations / concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | to 20 (count/read/write to 100) | +/− **within 10** automatic; number bonds to 10 | Part-whole, subitise to 6, place value (tens & ones), +/− within 20 |
| **G2** | to 1000 | +/− **within 20** automatic; ×2/×5/×10 emerging | 2-digit +/− with regrouping (concrete→abstract), skip-counting, intro multiplication as repeated addition |
| **G3** | to 10,000 | **all × & ÷ facts within 100** (tables to 10×10) | Formal ×/÷, unit fractions, 3-digit +/− fluent |
| **G4** | to 100,000+ | tables **fully automatic**; multi-digit +/− fluent | Multi-digit ×, ÷ with remainders, equivalent fractions, +/− fractions (like denominators) |
| **G5** | to millions; decimals | multi-digit × & ÷ fluent | Fraction ×/÷, decimals to hundredths, %, area/volume, multi-step word problems |

The through-line: **each grade's *new* operation depends on the prior grade's facts being *automatic*.** G4 long division is impossible if G3 tables aren't automatic — which is exactly the ASER failure signature (children stuck at recognition/subtraction never reach division). Fluency is not optional polish; it is the **load-bearing dependency** between grades.

# Part II — Pressure-test result: what each Maths lesson TYPE must contain

**Verdict: keep the five types; do NOT add a whole separate "fluency lesson"; DO make three elements mandatory inside every concept, and add one dedicated `fluency` *element* (a slot, not a lesson type).**

Rationale: the evidence wants number-sense, fact-fluency, and mastery-checks to be **daily and distributed**, not quarantined into one weekly "drill lesson." A separate lesson type would (a) mass what should be spaced, and (b) let the other four types off the hook. So they become **required elements every lesson carries**, sized differently per type.

### The three mandatory elements (every concept, every lesson)

1. **Number-sense warm-up (3-5 min)** — subitising / ten-frame / number-bond / Number-Talk, on the number range for this grade. Runs *before* the CPA content. This is the daily number-sense routine Part 2 demands.
2. **Fact-fluency drill (2-8 min)** — short, strategy-derived, low-stakes, self-referenced, spaced & interleaved retrieval of the facts this grade must automate (Part 3's left-hand column). Game-framed. **No whole-class timed test, ever.**
3. **Mastery check / exit gate (last 3-5 min)** — a quick "can they do it *and* explain it?" check with a **"what's the same, what's different?" variation** item. Advancing to the next concept is gated on this, per mastery (Part 4).

### Per-type specification (mapped to our taxonomy)

| Type | CPA role | Must contain | Number-sense warm-up | Fact-fluency drill | Mastery-check | Bilingual note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`concrete`** | Bruner **enactive** — build meaning with manipulatives | Physical manipulative tied *explicitly* to the concept (Dienes, ten-frame, counters, fraction bars); teacher models the concrete→quantity link | ✔ full (subitise/bond) | ✔ light (oral) | ✔ concrete check ("show me with the blocks") | Urdu encouraged for reasoning; English term introduced on the object |
| **`pictorial`** | Bruner **iconic** — same concept as picture/bar model | Bar model / number-line / array *of the same problem type*; no manipulative in hand but the picture mirrors it | ✔ full | ✔ (flashcards/game) | ✔ pictorial check | bilingual word wall; picture carries the term |
| **`pictorial_abstract`** | the **bridge** — picture *with* numerals/notation | Picture annotated with symbols; the notation introduced *only now*, on top of secure meaning; **variation** set (change one thing at a time) | ✔ full | ✔ **heaviest** (this is the automaticity slot) | ✔ + variation item | English notation is term-of-record; Urdu for meaning-negotiation |
| **`word_problem`** | **application / transfer** | 1-2 contextual problems; child states the *plan* then computes; explicit hunt for language traps (share/more/times-as-many); operation choice is the objective | ✔ (quick) | ✔ (embedded — facts used *in situ*) | ✔ "explain your choice of operation" | **highest language load**: Urdu for the plan, English for term + answer; check comprehension bilingually |
| **`revision`** | **spaced consolidation** — NOT "redo the exercises" | **Interleaved** spiral review pulling *earlier* concepts + this one; spaced retrieval of facts; error-analysis of the term's misconceptions | ✔ | ✔ **spaced/interleaved** (the durability slot) | ✔ cumulative check across the unit's SLOs | terms assessed in English; understanding may be checked bilingually |

### The fixed per-concept sequence (the CPA analogue, with elements)

```
       ┌──────────── one CONCEPT (small mastery step) ─────────────┐
[NS warm-up]→ concrete → pictorial → pictorial_abstract → word_problem →→ [MASTERY GATE] → next concept
              (build)    (see)       (bridge+notation+       (transfer)
                                       VARIATION practice)
   … with a FACT-FLUENCY drill riding on every day, and REVISION as the periodic
     spaced/interleaved spiral that revisits this concept days/weeks later.
```

- **Order is not negotiable within a concept:** never `pictorial_abstract` (bare notation) before `concrete` — that is the "memorise the algorithm with no referent" pathway that *manufactures* the Part-5 misconceptions.
- **`word_problem` comes after the notation is secure**, not before — a child can't choose an operation they can't yet perform.
- **`revision` decouples in time:** it is *scheduled later* (spacing), and it *interleaves* concepts — it is the taxonomy's home for the spaced-retrieval evidence. Do not let it degrade into "same exercises again."
- **Segment-index scheme (reuse the district verbatim, matching the Science lane):** `1-50` = sequential concept day; `900-949` = spiral/interleaved review (references earlier chapters); `990-999` = unit mastery review (`revision` summative).
- **SLO codes:** `M-0G-Ax-yy` = Maths – Grade – Strand/competency – SLO#, parsed from each source-curriculum chapter opener.

### Do we need a distinct "fluency/drill" lesson? — **No. A fluency ELEMENT, not a lesson type.**
- A separate weekly drill lesson **masses** practice the evidence says to **space** — it is the *less* effective arrangement (Part 3). 
- A distinct type also implies the *other* types skip fluency — the opposite of what we want.
- **Decision:** `fluency` is a **required element** present in all five types (heaviest in `pictorial_abstract` and `revision`), sized 2-8 min, never its own tagged lesson. If the divider must emit a fluency artefact, emit it as an **element/slot inside the day's lesson**, not a `skill_key`.

# Part III — The recommended G1-5 progression (number sense → operations → fluency → problem-solving)

The macro-progression each grade repeats, at that grade's number range:

```
NUMBER SENSE (daily, all year)  →  OPERATION built C→P→A  →  FACT FLUENCY automated (short/daily/spaced)  →  PROBLEM-SOLVING (word problems, transfer)  →  SPACED REVISION (interleaved spiral)
```

Where fact-fluency drilling fits, per grade (the load-bearing dependency):

| Grade | Number-sense focus | Operation built (C→P→A) | Facts to automate (the drill target) | Problem-solving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | subitise to 6; bonds to 10; place value tens/ones | +/− within 20 | **+/− within 10 automatic** | 1-step +/− stories |
| **G2** | bonds to 20; skip-count; 3-digit place value | 2-digit +/− with regrouping; × as repeated addition | **+/− within 20 automatic; ×2/5/10** | 1-2 step +/− with regrouping |
| **G3** | ×/÷ as array/grouping; unit fractions | formal ×/÷; 3-digit +/− | **all tables to 10×10 + related ÷ facts** | 2-step mixed-operation; unit-fraction |
| **G4** | equivalent fractions; multi-digit place value | multi-digit ×; ÷ with remainder; +/− fractions | **tables fully automatic; +/− bonds instant** | multi-step; fraction contexts |
| **G5** | decimals; %; magnitude to millions | fraction ×/÷; decimal ops; area/volume | **multi-digit × & ÷ procedures fluent** | multi-step, multi-operation, real-world |

**The rule that makes this work:** you may not advance a child to grade *N*'s new operation until grade *N-1*'s facts are automatic — because grade *N* consumes that automaticity as working-memory headroom. This is precisely where ASER children fall off the ladder; the progression's job is to hold the fluency gate closed until it's earned.

# Part IV — Concrete drilling/practice mechanics for a 25-30 min lesson (anxiety-safe)

A worked 30-minute Maths lesson skeleton that carries all three mandatory elements without inducing anxiety:

| Min | Segment | What happens | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0-5** | **Number-sense warm-up** | Flash a ten-frame / dot pattern → "how many? how did you see it?" (subitising + Number Talk). Or a quick number-bond round. Whole-class, oral, no writing, no marks. | Part 2 (number sense daily) |
| **5-10** | **Fact-fluency drill (anxiety-safe)** | *Beat-your-own-score* format: child does a short set of facts against **their own** last score, **untimed or generous time**, self-marks, celebrates growth. Or a **game** (fact-family bingo, "make ten" card game, dice). Interleave 2 fact types. **Never** a public timed test; **never** ranked. | Part 3 (short/frequent/low-stakes/spaced/interleaved) |
| **10-22** | **Core CPA teach** (the day's type) | The `concrete`/`pictorial`/`pictorial_abstract`/`word_problem` content — I-do (model) → we-do (guided) → you-do (independent). For `pictorial_abstract`, the **you-do set uses variation** (change one thing at a time; "what's the same, what's different?"). | Parts 1 & 4 (CPA + variation) |
| **22-27** | **Independent practice** | Small **varied** set (not 20 clones); teacher circulates, catches the Part-5 misconceptions live. | Part 4 (intelligent practice) |
| **27-30** | **Mastery-check exit gate** | 1-2 items *plus* "explain how you know"; a variation item. Result decides whether the class advances or the concept gets another day. | Part 4 (check before moving on) |

**Anxiety-safe design rules (make these LP-level constraints):**
1. **Self-referenced, never peer-ranked** — the child competes with their own previous score.
2. **Untimed or generous time** for anything graded; save *pace* for games where the stakes are fun, not marks.
3. **Mistakes are data, said out loud** — "great mistake, it shows us…" (growth-mindset framing; Boaler).
4. **Strategy before speed** — teach the *route* to the fact (make-ten, doubles, ×-relationships) before asking for quick recall.
5. **Game-frame the drill** — a game is retrieval practice the child *wants* to repeat, which is how spacing happens naturally.
6. **Bilingual safety net** — a child who knows the maths but lacks the English word is not marked wrong; check understanding, then re-pin the English term.

# Part V — Proficiency targets per grade (the LP's success criteria)

Restated as *assessable* targets an LP set should be able to demonstrate a child has hit (the through-line to the ASER ladder in bold):

- **End G1:** reads/writes numbers to 100; **+/− within 10 automatic**; number bonds to 10; can *show* a 2-digit number in tens & ones.
- **End G2:** **2-digit +/− with regrouping** (the ASER subtraction rung); +/− within 20 automatic; ×2/5/10; skip-counts.
- **End G3:** **all multiplication & division facts to 10×10 automatic**; formal ×/÷; unit fractions; 3-digit +/− fluent.
- **End G4:** **multi-digit × and ÷ with remainders** (the ASER division rung — the one 54% of Class 5 currently miss); equivalent + like-denominator fractions.
- **End G5:** fraction ×/÷, decimals to hundredths, %, area/volume, **multi-step multi-operation word problems** solved with an explained plan.

**Non-negotiable cross-grade criterion:** at each grade the prior grade's facts are *automatic* (retrievable in ≈3s without strategy), evidenced by the fluency element — because that automaticity is the working-memory budget the next grade spends.

# Part VI — Gaps between best practice and a typical textbook lesson

What a typical primary-maths **textbook page** does vs what the evidence requires (the delta our LP layer must add on top of the target curriculum book):

| Best practice (this report) | Typical textbook lesson | The gap our LP must close |
|---|---|---|
| Concept built **concrete-first** with a specific manipulative | Jumps to worked example in **symbols** | LP must **prepend a concrete stage** + name the manipulative and the concrete→quantity link |
| **Daily number-sense** warm-up (subitising, bonds, Number Talk) | Absent — page is all exercises | LP must **inject a 3-5 min warm-up** every lesson |
| **Short, spaced, low-stakes** fact fluency, strategy-derived | Either absent, or a **massed timed test** | LP must add a **2-8 min anxiety-safe drill**, spaced & interleaved, and *forbid* the timed test |
| Practice uses **variation** ("intelligent practice") | **20 near-identical** sums (mechanical repetition) | LP must **re-sequence practice** so one thing changes at a time + add "what's the same/different?" |
| **Mastery gate** before advancing | Page turns regardless of understanding | LP must add an **exit-check** and a "another day if not secure" instruction |
| Word problems checked for **language traps**, bilingual scaffold | English word problems, no vocabulary support | LP must add the **bilingual maths glossary + trap-word flags** |
| Misconceptions **pre-empted** at the concrete stage | Misconceptions surface **after**, uncorrected | LP must name the **likely misconception + its concrete pre-empt** per concept |
| Facts treated as the **load-bearing dependency** between grades | Fluency treated as incidental | LP progression must **hold the fluency gate** grade-to-grade |

**Bottom line:** the target curriculum Maths book supplies the *content and sequence*; our LP layer must supply the **pedagogy the page omits** — the concrete front-end, the daily number-sense routine, the anxiety-safe fluency drill, the variation in practice, the mastery gate, the bilingual language scaffold, and the misconception pre-empts. Those seven additions are the difference between a page that *presents* maths and a lesson that *builds* numeracy.

# Appendix B — Trap words in word problems (route to operation)
"share equally / split" → ÷ · "how many more / fewer / difference" → − · "times as many / groups of / each" → × · "in total / altogether / combined" → + · "left / remaining" → − · "per / for each" → × or ÷ (context). These relational phrases are where EAL learners misroute — flag them in every `word_problem`.
