## 0. The thesis in one paragraph

Primary science is **not** taught topic-by-fact the way a reading passage is walked competency-by-competency. The whole world's research-backed default for sequencing a science topic is the **BSCS 5E learning cycle** — Engage → Explore → Explain → Elaborate → Evaluate — and it is explicitly a **multi-day planning architecture, not a single-lesson template**. the source textbook's Science book is *already built on a 5E spine* (its "Adventure Begins / Discovery Playground / Gather and Grow / What's the Science? / Skill Sharpener" sections map one-to-one onto the 5Es). So the correct segmentation is: **one topic = one 5E micro-cycle = a fixed, ordered run of ~5 lesson subtypes**, exactly the way Maths runs Concrete→Pictorial→Abstract. On top of that spine, because these are **Urdu-mother-tongue children learning science in English**, every lesson carries an ESL/EAL layer — a bilingual key-term glossary, a translanguaging policy for *when* Urdu is allowed, and English pinned as the term-of-record — because the research is unambiguous that the science-vocabulary (CALP) load, not the concept, is where EAL learners fail. The taxonomy below (**Science-5**: `engage_hook → investigate_handson → concept_build → apply_connect → review_assess`) is that spine plus that layer, decided.

## Part I — The research base

### 1. The 5E instructional model (BSCS) — the spine

The **5E Instructional Model** was developed in 1987 by the Biological Sciences Curriculum Study (BSCS, Rodger Bybee et al.), building on the Atkin–Karplus "learning cycle" from the Science Curriculum Improvement Study. It is the dominant, research-validated sequence for teaching science and is the structural basis of most NGSS-aligned curricula. Its five phases:

| Phase | What the learner does | Teacher move |
|---|---|---|
| **Engage** | Surfaces prior ideas; curiosity is provoked | Short hook, discrepant event, question, story |
| **Explore** | Hands-on, first-hand experience *before* being told the answer | Facilitates an investigation; withholds the explanation |
| **Explain** | Concept + correct terminology are introduced *after* exploration | Names the phenomenon, formalises vocabulary |
| **Elaborate** | Applies/transfers the concept to a new context | Extension task, real-world connection |
| **Evaluate** | Demonstrates understanding | Formative checks throughout + a summative task |

Two findings from the literature matter for *how we segment*:

- **It is a multi-lesson architecture, not a lesson.** "The 5E model is not a single-lesson formula… typically spanning several class periods or even weeks. For older students, one phase might take several class periods." Using all five Es inside one 40-minute lesson "reduces the effectiveness of individual phases." This is the single most important segmentation fact: **a topic decomposes into multiple typed teaching days, and the phase order is the segmentation order.**
- **Phases can loop.** Engage comes first and Elaborate follows Explain, but Explore↔Explain can cycle more than once, and Evaluate runs throughout (e.g. Engage, Explore, Explain, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate).
- **Effectiveness is meta-analytically supported.** A 2024 systematic review & meta-analysis (Polanin et al., *AERA Open*) and a 2023 systematic review (*Int. J. Sci. Math. Educ.*) both find positive effects of 5E-based instruction on science achievement over conventional teaching.

Sources: [BSCS — Origins & Effectiveness](https://bscs.org/reports/the-bscs-5e-instructional-model-origins-and-effectiveness/) · [BSCS full report PDF](https://media.bscs.org/bscsmw/5es/bscs_5e_full_report.pdf) · [Systematic review, Springer 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10763-023-10357-y) · [Polanin et al. meta-analysis, AERA Open 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23328584241269866) · [SDCOE 5E model](https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/evidence-based-practices/5e-model-of-instruction) · [HMH — 5E explained](https://www.hmhco.com/blog/5e-instructional-model)

### 2. Inquiry-based science + POE (Predict–Observe–Explain)

The **Explore** phase is not "do a craft activity" — it is **structured inquiry**, and the highest-leverage, most classroom-portable structure for it is **POE (Predict–Observe–Explain)**, advanced by White & Gunstone (1992, *Probing Understanding*), itself a refinement of Champagne, Klopfer & Anderson's "Demonstrate–Observe–Explain" (1979). POE is a three-move constructivist routine:

1. **Predict** — the child commits to an outcome *and states a reason*, before seeing anything. This surfaces the misconception.
2. **Observe** — the child watches the demonstration/experiment and records what actually happens.
3. **Explain** — the child reconciles any gap between prediction and observation. The gap is where conceptual change happens.

POE is ideal for primary science because it (a) works with simple, immediately-observable demonstrations, (b) forces the child's own theory into the open where it can be revised, and (c) is language-light at the prediction stage — a child can predict in Urdu before having the English words. Meta-analytic and quasi-experimental studies report gains in achievement, attitude and retention from POE-structured lessons. **the source textbook's "Discovery Playground" activities are already POE-shaped** — each has an *Observe and Inquire* prompt that asks children to predict, then observe over time, then explain the difference (e.g. the three-pot plant-food experiment: sunlight+water vs sunlight-only vs water-only).

Sources: [White & Gunstone POE, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265847902_Predict_Observe_Explain_Activities_enhancing_scientific_understanding) · [POE effects on achievement/attitude/retention (ERIC EJ1341654)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1341654.pdf) · [NZCER Assessment Resource Banks — POE](https://arbs.nzcer.org.nz/predict-observe-explain-poe)

### 3. The ESL/EAL layer — this is where these learners actually fail

The children in scope are **Urdu (or Punjabi/Sindhi/Pashto) mother-tongue learners doing science in English or bilingually**. The research says the concept is rarely the blocker; the **language of science is**. Three literatures converge:

**(a) BICS vs CALP (Cummins).** Conversational fluency (**BICS**, Basic Interpersonal Communicative Skills) develops in ~1-2 years; the **academic** language of textbooks and science talk (**CALP**, Cognitive Academic Language Proficiency) — abstract, context-reduced, specialised — takes **5-7 years**. A child can *sound* fluent and still not hold "photosynthesis," "germination," "dispersal." **Segmentation implication: the vocabulary is not a side-note in the Explain lesson; it is a first-class object that must be pre-taught, glossed bilingually, and revisited.**
Sources: [Colorín Colorado — BICS/CALP](https://www.colorincolorado.org/faq/what-are-bics-and-calp) · [NSW Dept of Education — BICS & CALP (PDF)](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/multicultural-education/eald/urh/What_are_BICS_and_CALP.pdf) · [Cummins, BICS/CALP status (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226699482_BICS_and_CALP_Empirical_and_theoretical_status_of_the_distinction)

**(b) CLIL + translanguaging.** In Content-and-Language-Integrated-Learning (CLIL) science classrooms, the evidence-backed practice is **pedagogical translanguaging** — the *strategic, planned* use of L1 (Urdu) alongside L2 (English) to negotiate meaning, foreground technical vocabulary, and let children reason with their full linguistic repertoire. Findings: students using translanguaging **outperform English-only peers** on content because they handle language-related comprehension challenges better; translanguaging builds **bilingual vocabulary** and metalinguistic awareness. The recommended CLIL model bundles **Dual Language Input, Visual/Multimodal Support, Contextualised Vocabulary Instruction, Collaborative Meaning-Making, and Reflective Assessment**. Concretely, teachers: allow children to describe a phenomenon in their own words/L1, give bilingual translations of key terms, use body language, visuals and labelled diagrams, and reserve L1 for meaning-negotiation while keeping the target term in L2.
Sources: [Functions of Translanguaging in Primary CLIL/EFL (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388614966_Functions_of_Translanguaging_in_Primary_School_CLIL_and_EFL_Settings) · [Pedagogical translanguaging in CLIL (ScienceDirect 2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000088) · [Decade of scaffolding in CLIL science (John Benjamins)](https://benjamins.com/catalog/jicb.23010.nas) · [CLIL, Cambridge Elements](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/content-and-language-integrated-learning-clil/9F6F698B7526AC6313DA1E95A61C2271)

**(c) The Pakistan reality.** Public schools in Punjab were Urdu-medium until 2010, then declared English-medium; national policy since 2009 keeps **English as the medium for science and maths** while allowing Urdu elsewhere. Research on Lahore public primaries finds EMI is a **major barrier** for L1-Urdu/Punjabi/Saraiki/Pashto learners — it lowers motivation and confidence, and most students believe they would perform better if taught in Urdu. This is not an argument to abandon English (English science terms are the currency of secondary school and exams); it is the argument for **bilingual scaffolding with English as the deliberate floor** — precisely the project's existing Language Protocol (Rule 20: English is the floor, never the ceiling; never ship a partial map).
Sources: [Language Barrier at Public Primary School, Lahore (Zenodo)](https://zenodo.org/records/17728944) · [The English/Urdu-Medium Divide in Pakistan (ERIC EJ1216773)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1216773.pdf)

### 4. How primary science differs by grade — G4 concrete/observational vs G5 more abstract

Children aged ~9-11 are in Piaget's **concrete operational** stage: they reason logically but **only about tangible, observable things they can see or manipulate**; systematic, hypothetical, fully abstract reasoning (formal operations) begins ~11-12. The transition is gradual and *within* the G4-G5 band:

- **G4 (~age 9) — observational/descriptive.** Center of gravity: *observe, describe, classify, record change over time, name parts.* Investigations are single-variable and qualitative ("which pot grew, which wilted"). Concepts are introduced concretely and anchored to a diagram or a physical object. Bloom center: Remember → Understand, with Apply on the hands-on.
- **G5 (~age 10-11) — explanatory/relational.** Center of gravity: *explain mechanisms, compare, reason about cause-and-effect, run a fair test with a controlled variable, read a simple data table.* The same 5E spine is used, but `investigate_handson` adds **variable control / fair-testing** and `concept_build` pushes into **mechanism and abstraction** (why, not just what). Bloom center shifts up to Understand → Analyze, with Evaluate/Create in the elaborate/assess.

Practical rule for the divider: **the five skill_types are identical across G4 and G5; the Bloom ceiling and the fair-test rigor rise at G5.** Do not invent a different taxonomy per grade.
Sources: [Piaget, Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/piaget.html) · [Concrete operational stage (Simply Psychology)](https://www.simplypsychology.org/concrete-operational.html) · [Middle-childhood cognition, Baylor OpenBooks](https://openbooks.library.baylor.edu/lifespanhumandevelopment/chapter/middle-childhood-cognition/)

## Part II — THE SCIENCE-5 TAXONOMY (decided)

The analogue of Maths' `concrete → pictorial → abstract`. **Five skill_types run in a fixed pedagogical order per topic** (a 5E micro-cycle). Each teaching-day lesson is tagged with exactly **one dominant** skill_type (it may absorb a short adjacent section — e.g. a one-page hook folds into the investigate lesson).

| # | `skill_key` | Display name | 5E phase | What it does | When used | Typical Bloom (G4 → G5) | Per chapter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `engage_hook` | Engage & Hook | **Engage** | Story/comic hook + a driving question; surfaces prior ideas and misconceptions; sets the topic's "why". Language-light — safe to think aloud in Urdu here. | Opens the chapter and opens each topic (short; usually folded into the first investigate lesson after the chapter opener). | Understand (Remember) | **1 dedicated** (chapter orientation); per-topic hooks fold in |
| 2 | `investigate_handson` | Investigate (Hands-on) | **Explore** | The POE inquiry: **Predict → Observe → Explain-the-gap** using the activity's Materials/Procedure; records data over time. First-hand experience *before* the answer. | Once per topic. The load-bearing lesson. Long-running observations (days/weeks) run in the background across subsequent lessons. | Apply / Analyze (G5 adds fair-test + variable control) | **~1 per topic** (≈3-4) |
| 3 | `concept_build` | Concept Build (What's the Science?) | **Explain** | Formalises the concept *after* exploration; introduces + defines the **key terms** (the vocab box). This is the CALP lesson — the bilingual glossary lives here. Ends with a quick check. | Once per topic, immediately after its investigation. | Understand (G5 → Analyze: mechanism/cause-effect) | **~1 per topic** (≈3-4) |
| 4 | `apply_connect` | Apply & Connect | **Elaborate** | Transfers the concept to a new/real-world context — project, skit, "design/create", cross-topic synthesis, "jobs related to plants", Fun-Facts/Scientist-Spotlight extension. | Once per chapter (near the end), after concepts are built. | Apply / Create | **1 per chapter** |
| 5 | `review_assess` | Review & Assess | **Evaluate** | Consolidation + summative check across the chapter's SLOs. Absorbs the per-topic *Skill Sharpener* checks (formative) and the end-of-chapter *Mastery Challenge* (summative, spans Remember→Create). | Per-topic quick-checks fold into `concept_build`; one dedicated chapter-review lesson at the end. Also the spiral-review slot. | Evaluate (full Bloom range) | **1 per chapter** (+ folded formative checks) |

### The fixed sequence (the CPA analogue)

```
        ┌──────────────── one TOPIC = one 5E micro-cycle ────────────────┐
CHAPTER OPEN → [ engage_hook → investigate_handson → concept_build ] ×(each topic) → apply_connect → review_assess
  (Explorer's        Engage          Explore              Explain              Elaborate         Evaluate
   Pathway/SLO map)
```

- **Order is not negotiable** within a topic: you never `concept_build` before `investigate_handson` (that would be "tell then confirm," which the 5E research shows is *less* effective than "explore then explain").
- **Long-running observations decouple from the daily sequence.** `investigate_handson` for a growth/germination experiment is *set up* on one teaching day but *observed* across 1-2 weeks — the "Gather and Grow" record table runs in the background while later topics proceed. The divider should tag the setup day as `investigate_handson` and note the observation window in the lesson's `Comments`.
- **Segment-Index scheme (reuse the district verbatim):** `1-50` = sequential content day within the chapter; `900-949` = spiral review (references earlier chapters); `990-999` = chapter review (`review_assess`/Mastery Challenge).
- **SLO codes:** `S-04-A1-01` = Subject(S) – Grade(04) – Strand/competency(A1) – SLO#(01). The strand codes come from each chapter's **Explorer's Pathway** SLO-map; build a source-curriculum Science SLO catalog from those openers.

## Part III — The ESL/Urdu layer, baked into every lesson

The Language Protocol (root Rule 20) applies: **English is the floor, Urdu is the scaffold, never a partial map.** Concretely, every Science lesson the divider produces carries these fields/behaviours:

### 3.1 Bilingual key-term glossary (the non-negotiable)
Every `concept_build` lesson (and the term when first introduced in an `engage_hook`/`investigate_handson`) attaches a glossary row per key term:

`{ term_en, term_ur (Urdu gloss / script), everyday_ur_bridge, keep_english: true|false }`

- **`term_en`** — the English science term is the **term of record** (it's what secondary school + exams use). Always shown.
- **`term_ur`** — an Urdu gloss in Nastaliq for meaning. For internationally-standard technical tokens (oxygen, carbon dioxide, xylem, phloem) use the **transliteration + a one-line Urdu function gloss**, not a forced neologism — `keep_english: true`.
- **`everyday_ur_bridge`** — the BICS→CALP bridge: an everyday Urdu analogy the child already owns ("plants are little chefs cooking their own food" → کھانا خود پکاتے ہیں). The book already does this ("Imagine plants are little chefs") — extend it into Urdu.

### 3.2 Translanguaging moves per skill_type (when L1 is allowed)
| skill_type | L1 (Urdu) policy | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `engage_hook` | **Urdu encouraged.** Predict, wonder, argue in Urdu. | Language-light phase; lowering the language barrier maximises engagement + surfaces real prior ideas (BICS is fine here). |
| `investigate_handson` | **Urdu for prediction + group talk; English key terms labelled.** "Say your prediction in Urdu, write the label in English." | POE prediction must not be gated on English; but the term gets pinned in L2. Collaborative meaning-making (CLIL). |
| `concept_build` | **Bilingual — L2 term introduced, L1 used to negotiate meaning, then L2 reinforced.** Bilingual word wall. | This is the CALP moment; dual-language input + contextualised vocabulary instruction. |
| `apply_connect` | **English foregrounded, Urdu allowed for reasoning.** | Transfer/output; push productive L2 use with L1 as a safety net. |
| `review_assess` | **Terms assessed in English; comprehension may be checked bilingually.** | Exam alignment needs English; but don't fail a child who knows the science and lacks the English word — check understanding, then re-pin the term. |

### 3.3 Visual + multimodal support (mandatory, not optional)
Labelled diagrams, the recurring characters (Gul Rukh, Rani, Jojo), and the vocab box are the CLIL "visual scaffold." Every key term should ride on a picture. This is already the book's design — preserve it in the LP.

## Part V — Worked example: "Green Guardians of Earth" (G4 Ch1, printed pp. 1-28)

**Chapter shape (from real page-truth):** 1 Explorer's-Pathway opener with **7 SLOs**, then **4 topics** each running the full section cycle, then a chapter-wide Mastery Challenge + a Connect-and-Create project.

- SLOs (from the Explorer's Pathway path): (1) explain how plants/living things use air, water, food & space; (2) classify plants into groups w/ examples; (3) investigate how plants use water; (4) explain the life cycle of flowering plants (seeds→new plants); (5) explore the role of flowers in making seeds; (6) understand why plants are essential for life on Earth; (7) discover plant-related jobs.
- Topics: **T1 What Do Plants Eat?** (pp.2-9, photosynthesis) · **T2 Transport of Water in Plants** (pp.10-14, xylem/phloem) · **T3 Types of Plants** (pp.15-19, flowering/non-flowering + flower parts) · **T4 What Does a Seed Need to Awaken?** (pp.20-25, germination + life cycle).

**Type distribution for this chapter:** `engage_hook` ×1 (dedicated; three more hooks folded), `investigate_handson` ×4, `concept_build` ×4, `apply_connect` ×1, `review_assess` ×1 → **11 lessons**. At ~3 science periods/week this is a ~4-week chapter — consistent with the S&S sizing bootstrap (Sci ~3/wk, ~34 content weeks).

**G5 delta (for the same taxonomy):** at G5 the `investigate_handson` lessons add an explicit **fair-test / controlled-variable** frame and a data-table read; `concept_build` pushes to **mechanism** (why xylem pulls water, why chlorophyll matters) — the Bloom ceiling rises to Analyze/Evaluate without changing the five skill_types.
