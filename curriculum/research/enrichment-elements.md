#### Every lesson, every subject
Open with a **2–6 min cumulative spaced-retrieval warm-up** — retrieve *prior* lessons, not just this
one. Retrieval + spacing combined is the single strongest learning-science lever, and it is the thing
textbook teaching under-uses most (one revision lesson at unit end is massing what must be spaced).
Bilingual rule: the medium-of-instruction term is the **term-of-record and the floor**; L1 is the
meaning-negotiation scaffold; **never ship a partial map**.

#### English — Science of Reading
The Simple View is a **product**: `Reading Comprehension = Decoding × Language Comprehension`. For an
L2 learner **both factors start near zero**, so both must be built deliberately and in parallel.

| Type | Must contain |
|---|---|
| `phonics` | spaced review drill → explicit new grapheme–**phoneme** teaching (sounds, not letter names; SATPIN order, not A–Z) + articulation/minimal-pair note where the phoneme is **absent in L1** → **blend/decode** → **segment + ENCODE (dictation)** → **decodable connected text (≥95% known patterns)** → retrieval exit check. *Encoding and decodable text are the two biggest gaps.* |
| `writing` | **dictation/encoding, not copying.** Copying from the board is NOT encoding. |
| `oral_communication` | **load-bearing for L2, not filler** — structured oral production, pronunciation of L2-only phonemes, listening comprehension. This builds the multiplier reading depends on. |
| `pre_reading` | activate background knowledge (L1 fine) + **pre-teach 3–6 words** to get the text to ~95–98% known + set a purpose. |
| `reading_comprehension` | **fluency-first repeated reading** (model → choral → echo → partner) *then* an explicit strategy taught I-do/we-do/you-do, literal **and** inferential questions. |
| `vocabulary_grammar` | child-friendly definition + example + non-example + use it; morphology from G3+; grammar taught **orally first**, in real sentences. |
| `revision` | cumulative, **interleaved** retrieval; timed self-competition only once accuracy is secure. |

**Drill dosage:** short (2–6 min), **>90% success**, spaced across days, **retrieval** not re-reading,
untimed until accurate → then timed self-competition. **Targets (WCPM, aspirational ceiling; set an
L2-realistic interim band ~70–80%):** G1~60 · G2~100 · G3~112 · G4~133 · G5~146. **Track the child's
ACTUAL decoding level** — a G5 class spans G1–G5 readers, so LPs need a low-floor entry.
**Never teach sight words as whole-shape memorisation** — map them through their sounds.

#### Urdu — the same science, two script-specific twists
- **Aeraab/diacritics PRESENT and explicitly taught G1–3.** Pointed Urdu is a *shallow* (decodable)
  orthography; unpointed Urdu is deep and pushes beginners into contextual guessing — the exact habit
  that caps them later. Fade by frequency, not a hard grade line: fully pointed G1–2 → partial G3 →
  near-unpointed G4–5, with pointing returning for any new hard word.
- **Letter-form + RAN load is unusually high.** Many graphemes differ only by dots, and every letter
  has up to **four positional shapes**. RAN is the strongest predictor of Urdu reading fluency. So:
  `arkaan_saazi` = print-free phonemic awareness → grapheme with **all 4 positional forms** →
  **dot-discrimination drill** → aeraab → blend pointed syllables, plus a **daily ~5-min spaced
  automaticity flash**.
- **آدھی اشکال (half-forms) ARE Urdu's blending mechanism** — harder than English CVC because the
  child transforms the *glyph*, not just the sound. The **~9 non-joiners (ا د ڈ ذ ر ڑ ز ژ و)** have
  no left half-form and break the chain into ligature chunks — teach WITH joining, not separately.
  The Qaida **توڑ/جوڑ (break/join)** drill is evidence-aligned glyph-level segment↔blend; keep it and
  make it a **production loop**: `arkaan_saazi` carries `torDrill`/`jorDrill` + an `isNonJoiner`
  chain-break rule. Targets: half-form recognition **secure end-G1, automatic end-G2**.
- `buland_khwani` = repeated reading of **decodable POINTED** text + track cwpm; **it also owns
  SPEAKING**. `tafheem` **owns LISTENING comprehension** + a literal→inferential ladder.
  `takhleeqi_likhai` adds the missing **encoding tier** — تختی handwriting in all four positions +
  a 2–3-min pointed **املا/dictation every lesson**, graded with
  `markScheme:"connected-form-correct"` (grade the CONNECTED form, not isolated letters — the
  neglected half). `alfaaz_maani` = oral-first vocab. `duhrai` = spaced retrieval with prior items
  resurfacing.
- **The oral question is CLOSED: keep 7 types, NO standalone oral type.** Urdu here is the children's
  **L1** — oral language is abundant; the scarce, school-only skills are decoding, fluency and
  encoding. An 8th oral slot would steal 25–30 minutes from exactly the gap the national assessment
  measures. Speaking folds into `buland_khwani`, listening into `tafheem`; add an **oral-first
  opening beat** inside `takhleeqi_likhai` and `alfaaz_maani` (say-before-write / say-before-read).
  (D-013.)
- **Targets (cwpm):** G2~30–50 · G3~55–70 · G4~70–85 · G5≈90 (the national proficiency benchmark).

#### Maths — CPA is right; three elements are missing
Keep the five CPA types. **Do NOT add a separate drill lesson — that masses what must be spaced.**
Instead every concept carries three mandatory elements:
1. **Number-sense warm-up (3–5 min)** — subitising / ten-frame / number-bond / Number Talk. Early
   number sense is the strongest single predictor of later maths achievement, and gaps **widen** if
   unaddressed.
2. **Anxiety-safe fact-fluency drill (2–8 min)** — the drilling debate resolves cleanly:
   **short & frequent · strategy-first then automate · low-stakes and self-referenced (child races
   their own last score) · spaced & interleaved · game-framed.** **NEVER a whole-class timed test** —
   for about a third of students timed testing is the onset of maths anxiety, and stress blocks the
   working memory the facts live in.
3. **Mastery-check exit gate (3–5 min)** — "can they do it *and* explain it?", including a
   **variation** item ("what's the same? what's different?"). Advancing is gated on it.

Plus: **practice uses variation, not 20 near-identical sums** (change one thing at a time); a
**named misconception + its concrete pre-empt** per concept (place-value regrouping as memorised
digit-moves; subtraction reverting to addition; "bigger denominator = bigger fraction"; adding
numerators *and* denominators; "=" meaning "the answer is"); and a **bilingual glossary + trap-word
flags** for word problems ("share equally"→÷, "how many more"→−, "times as many"→×) — the word
problem is where **language**, not maths, causes failure for an L2 learner.
**Load-bearing rule:** do not advance to grade N's operation until grade N−1's facts are automatic —
that automaticity is the working-memory budget grade N spends. **Targets:** G1 ±within10 automatic ·
G2 2-digit ± with regrouping · G3 all tables to 10×10 · G4 multi-digit ×÷ with remainders · G5
fraction ×÷, decimals, multi-step. `fluency` is an **element/slot, never a `skill_key`**.

#### Science — 5E fidelity + the ESL layer
`investigate_handson` runs **POE (Predict → Observe → Explain)**: the child commits to an outcome
*and a reason* before seeing anything, which surfaces the misconception where it can be revised.
`concept_build` is the **CALP lesson** and owns the bilingual glossary — `{term_en, term_ur,
everyday_bridge, keep_english}`; internationally-standard tokens (oxygen, xylem) keep the English
term + a one-line function gloss rather than a forced neologism. **Translanguaging policy per phase:**
L1 encouraged at `engage_hook`; L1 for prediction and group talk at `investigate_handson` with the
term pinned in L2; bilingual at `concept_build`; L2 foregrounded at `apply_connect`; terms assessed
in L2 at `review_assess` but **don't fail a child who knows the science and lacks the word** — check
understanding, then re-pin the term. Visual/multimodal support (labelled diagrams, the recurring
cast, the vocab box) is **mandatory, not decoration**.

#### Low-resource reality (applies to every subject)
Hands-on means **materials a government school actually has** — slates, bottles, stones, old bread,
chalk — or a talk-through observation. Never microscopes, projectors, or per-pupil printed
materials. This changes the Explanation for every resource-dependent lesson, including maths
concrete work. (Operator law L6.)
