## TL;DR (the decided calls)

1. **The Science of Reading transfers to Urdu** — phonological/phonemic awareness and systematic, explicit phonics are the causal spine, confirmed *in Urdu specifically* (U-PASS longitudinal study). But Urdu adds two non-negotiable local twists: **(a) diacritics (aeraab) must be present and explicitly taught in G1–3** because they are what convert Urdu from a "deep" (opaque) to a "shallow" (transparent) orthography; **(b) letter-form recognition + Rapid Automatized Naming (RAN) carry unusually heavy predictive weight** because Nastaliq's position-variant, dot-disambiguated shapes make the *visual* decoding load real, not incidental.
2. **The traditional قاعدہ (Qaida) is not wrong — it is incomplete.** Its sequence (letter shapes → aeraab → joining → words) is exactly right and evidence-aligned. What it lacks is (i) explicit *phonemic-awareness* work divorced from print (hearing/blending/segmenting sounds), (ii) connected *decodable* text so decoding is practised in context, and (iii) *comprehension + oral language* as parallel strands. Keep the Qaida spine; bolt the missing three on.
3. **Oral-type verdict: DO NOT add a standalone oral/listening-speaking type. Fold it into `buland_khwani`, and make `tafheem` carry listening-comprehension.** Rationale below (§5) — for L1 Urdu, oral language is a *medium* woven through every type, not a scarce competency to protect with its own slot the way it is for English (an L2). Adding an 8th type risks the divider starving decoding/writing time to fill an "oral" quota the child already over-has.
4. **The crisis is real in Urdu too.** ASER: **>55% of rural Grade-5 children cannot read a Grade-2 Urdu text; >40% of Grade-3 children cannot identify individual letters.** The Pakistan Reading Project moved Grade-2 Urdu oral reading fluency from ~30 → 57 correct words/min (cwpm) with explicit method; the proficiency benchmark is ~90 cwpm. Our lessons must be measured against *decoding + fluency gains*, not coverage.

## Part I — HOW URDU LITERACY IS BEST TAUGHT

### 1.1 The Science of Reading, and that it transfers to Urdu

The evidence base ("Science of Reading") rests on two models. The **Simple View of Reading**: `Word Recognition × Language Comprehension = Reading Comprehension` — a product, so a zero on either side zeros the whole. And **Scarborough's Reading Rope**, which unpacks each: word-recognition = phonological awareness + decoding/phonics + sight recognition; language-comprehension = vocabulary + background knowledge + language structures + verbal reasoning + literacy knowledge ([Lexia — SoR breakdown](https://www.lexialearning.com/blog/a-full-breakdown-of-the-science-of-reading-components)). The instructional corollaries are settled: **systematic, explicit phonics** and **phonemic-awareness training taught with letters, in small focused doses**, sequenced simple→complex and cumulative ([Lexia](https://www.lexialearning.com/blog/a-full-breakdown-of-the-science-of-reading-components); [NWEA — teaching decoding](https://www.nwea.org/blog/2025/what-the-science-of-reading-tells-us-about-how-to-teach-decoding-including-phonics/)).

**Does it transfer to Urdu?** Yes — and this is now demonstrated *in Urdu*, not merely assumed. The **Urdu Phonological Tele-Assessment (U-PASS)** longitudinal study found **Urdu phonological awareness is a consistent longitudinal predictor of Urdu word/non-word reading accuracy and fluency**, and that PA "accounted for greater variance in reading accuracy … particularly in Urdu" ([Frontiers — U-PASS concurrent validity](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1572807/full); [ASHA/LSHSS — predictive validity](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2025_LSHSS-24-00131)). So phonemic awareness → phonics → decoding → fluency → comprehension is the correct spine for Urdu.

### 1.2 What is Urdu-specific (and why the Qaida survives, upgraded)

Two script facts change the emphasis, not the model:

- **Deep orthography by default, shallow when pointed.** Standard printed Urdu **omits short-vowel diacritics**, so the same consonant skeleton maps to several spoken words — the reader must *supply* the vowel from context. This makes unpointed Urdu a **deep/opaque orthography** and pushes beginners toward *contextual guessing rather than phonetic decoding* — the exact habit that later caps them ([Dawn — the case for teaching diacritics](https://www.dawn.com/news/1924375); [PMC — Predictors of Reading in Urdu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303915/)). **When diacritics are consistently present, "Urdu effectively becomes a shallow orthography"** and decoding becomes teachable the SoR way ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1924375)). Design consequence: **G1–3 text and drills must be fully pointed (با اعراب); aeraab is a taught object, not decoration.**
- **Visual/naming load is unusually high.** In Urdu, "many graphemes look similar or even identical and can only be discriminated by the presence of, the number of, or positioning of, dots," and letters take different **shapes by position**. Consequently **Rapid Automatized Naming (RAN) is the strongest predictor of Urdu reading fluency and accuracy** — stronger than in shallow Latin orthographies — supporting the orthographic-depth hypothesis that *visual processing becomes crucial in opaque systems* ([PMC — Predictors of Reading in Urdu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303915/)). Design consequence: **fast, accurate, automatic letter-*form* naming (all four positional shapes) and dot-discrimination must be drilled to automaticity — this is the arkaan/RAN engine, and it is not optional.**

**The traditional قاعدہ.** The Qaida teaches, in order: letter shapes (حروفِ تہجی) → vowel marks/harakat (aeraab) → **letter joining** (متّصل vs منفصل — which letters join left and which don't) → words → short pointed sentences ([Qaida method overview](https://learningquranonline.com/noorani-qaida-vs-other-qaida-methods-complete-comparison/)). Phonics-first Qaida variants have children **produce the sound before naming the letter** — which is exactly the SoR grapheme–phoneme move ([ibid.](https://learningquranonline.com/noorani-qaida-vs-other-qaida-methods-complete-comparison/)). **Verdict: the Qaida sequence is evidence-aligned and we keep it.** Its three gaps, which our lesson types must fill:
1. **Print-free phonemic awareness** — hearing, blending, segmenting, deleting *sounds* (اردو کی آوازیں) before/beside the letter, in small daily doses. Qaida jumps straight to print.
2. **Connected decodable text** — Qaida drills words in lists; children need *pointed connected sentences/stories restricted to taught letters* so decoding is rehearsed in context and automaticity + sight-word internalisation build ([re-reading decodable text evidence](https://www.nwea.org/blog/2025/supporting-fluency-and-comprehension-using-practices-grounded-in-the-science-of-reading/)).
3. **Comprehension + oral language as parallel strands from day 1** — the Qaida is silent on meaning; the Rope says language-comprehension is built in parallel, not after decoding is "finished."

## Part II — URDU DECODING MECHANICS (what "decode Nastaliq" actually means)

A G1 child must acquire, roughly in this order (this is the atomic content the `arkaan_saazi` / joining / phonics types deliver):

1. **The alphabet (~38–40 letters, حروفِ تہجی).** Includes the aspirated-consonant digraphs formed with دو چشمی ھ (بھ، پھ، تھ …) and the retroflex/nasal set (ٹ ڈ ڑ ں) that don't exist in Arabic — these are *Urdu-specific sounds* the phonemic-awareness strand must isolate by ear.
2. **The four positional forms (اشکال): initial / medial / final / isolated.** A letter is one *phoneme* but up to four *shapes*. Children must recognise all four as the same letter — this is a core RAN/visual task and a frequent early failure point.
3. **Dot-discrimination.** ب پ ت ٹ ث differ only by dots; ج چ ح خ share a skeleton. Explicit "same skeleton, count/position the dots" drills.
4. **Joining behaviour (حروفِ مُتّصل vs مُنفصل).** Most letters join both sides; the ~10 "non-connectors" (ا د ڈ ذ ر ڑ ز ژ و) join only to the right and force a break. This governs where words visually split into ligature-groups and is essential to reading Nastaliq's stacked ligatures.
5. **Aeraab / diacritics as the vowel system.** زبر (a), زیر (i), پیش (u); جزم/سکون (no vowel — closes a syllable); شد/تشدید (gemination — double the consonant); تنوین (nunation ً ٍ ٌ). **In G1–3 these are printed and decoded explicitly**; the child sounds بَ + تَ = بَتَ. This is what makes Urdu shallow-and-decodable for beginners ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1924375)).
6. **Syllable blending → words → pointed sentences.** CV, CVC, then multi-syllable, always pointed at first.

**When to drop the aeraab.** Aeraab is *scaffolding*, and unpointed text is the adult target, so it must fade — but only after decoding is automatic. Sequence the fade by *frequency*, not a hard grade line: **fully pointed G1–2; partial pointing (retain aeraab only on new/ambiguous words) across G3; near-unpointed connected text by G4–5** with pointing returning for any newly-introduced hard word. Dropping aeraab too early is precisely what breeds the "contextual-guessing, non-decoding" reader ASER later catches ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1924375)).

## Part III — FLUENCY & DRILLING

**بلند خوانی / oral reading fluency (ORF)** is the bridge from decoding to comprehension and the thing benchmarks actually measure (cwpm). The evidence-based mechanics we must bake into lessons:

- **Repeated reading.** Re-reading the *same* short pointed passage 3–4× lifts fluency on that text *and transfers to new texts*; it internalises sight words and gives accurate-but-choppy decoders smoothness ([Wymer — repeated readings](https://openjournals.utoledo.edu/index.php/learningtoteach/article/download/547/365/2605); [PLD — re-reading vs continuous](https://support.pld-literacy.org/en-au/article/re-reading-vs-continuous-reading-for-fluency-what-does-the-evidence-say-18k20t7/)). Pair with **echo/choral reading and partner reading** (teacher models a pointed line → class repeats).
- **Decodable text, not levelled guessing text.** Passages restricted to already-taught letters + aeraab build automaticity on known words and give clean practice decoding new ones ([NWEA — fluency & comprehension](https://www.nwea.org/blog/2025/supporting-fluency-and-comprehension-using-practices-grounded-in-the-science-of-reading/)).
- **Spaced retrieval for letters + high-frequency sight words.** Reviewing an item *just as it's about to be forgotten* beats massed repetition (Ebbinghaus; hundreds of replications), and generalises to new contexts ([Lexia — fluency activities](https://www.lexialearning.com/blog/the-science-of-reading-fluency-activities-for-students); [PMC — spaced retrieval word learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8084525/)). Practically: a **daily fast letter/joining/sight-word flash drill** where yesterday's + last-week's items resurface, not just today's.
- **How much drilling?** Small and daily beats long and occasional: a **~5-minute automaticity warm-up every lesson** (positional-form naming + dot-discrimination + blending) is the RAN engine; **fluency re-reading ~5–8 min** on a pointed passage. Automaticity — not one-time correctness — is the target, because RAN (speed) predicts Urdu reading more than any single skill ([PMC — Predictors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303915/)).

## Part IV — GRADE-LEVEL PROFICIENCY BENCHMARKS

**The ASER Urdu ladder** (the national measuring stick; a Grade-2-level task) has 5 rungs: `0 = cannot recognise letters → 1 = letter → 2 = word → 3 = short paragraph (sentences) → 4 = story` ([ASER validation tools](https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Aser%20survey/Tools%20validating_the_aser_testing_tools__oct_2012__2.pdf); [PAL Network — ASER Pakistan](https://palnetwork.org/aser-pakistan/)). A child is credited at the **story** rung if they read a Grade-2 story fluently with ≤3 errors.

**The crisis, in Urdu specifically:** in 2018 only **56% of Grade-5 children** could read a Grade-2 Urdu/Sindhi/Pashto story ([Inroads — Why young Pakistanis don't read](https://inroadsjournal.ca/why-young-pakistanis-dont-read/)); ASER framing: **>55% of rural Grade-5 cannot read a Grade-2 Urdu text and >40% of Grade-3 cannot identify letters** ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1924375)). EGRA in Sindh found Grade-3 children struggling to read *a single word* of Urdu/Sindhi ([Sindh EGRA baseline](https://rsusindh.gov.pk/contents/SELECT/EGRA_Baseline_Assessment_Report_Sindh.pdf)).

**Fluency numbers:** Pakistan adopted an ORF proficiency benchmark of **~90 cwpm in Urdu** (80 cwpm Sindhi) ([ACR-Asia EGRA benchmarks](https://ierc-publicfiles.s3.amazonaws.com/public/resources/Asia%20ACR%20Benchmarking%20Report_FINAL%20REVISED_21%20Dec%2017.pdf)). The Pakistan Reading Project raised Grade-2 Urdu ORF from **~30 cwpm (2014) → 57 cwpm (2017)** with explicit method — proof the gap is instructional, not innate ([Creative Associates — PRP](https://www.creativeassociatesinternational.com/story/pakistan-reading-project-wins-2020-international-prize-for-literacy-program-of-the-year/)).

**Our target ladder (decision — anchored to ASER rungs + the 90-cwpm ceiling):**

| End of grade | Reading (decode + fluency) | Writing (encode) |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | ASER rung 1→2. All letters incl. positional forms + aeraab; blend CV/CVC pointed syllables; read pointed 2–4-word strings. Emergent ORF (accuracy over speed). | Form all letters (تختی); write own name + pointed words from dictation (املا). |
| **G2** | ASER rung 2→3. Read fully-pointed short sentences/paragraph; **~30–50 cwpm** on a pointed G2 passage; joining fluent. | Pointed-word & short-sentence dictation; copy a sentence legibly. |
| **G3** | ASER rung 3→4. Read a short *story*; partial-pointing tolerated; **~55–70 cwpm**; retells the gist (tafheem). | Sentence-level free writing; spell common words unpointed; paragraph املا. |
| **G4** | ASER rung 4 (story) secure on **near-unpointed** text; **~70–85 cwpm**; answers inferential questions. | Short paragraph (تخلیقی لکھائی); punctuation; guided composition. |
| **G5** | Reads unpointed grade text fluently; **≈90 cwpm** (proficient); comprehends + summarises. | Multi-paragraph composition, letter/story writing, self-editing. |

(Grade cwpm bands interpolate the PRP G2≈57 data point up to the 90-cwpm proficiency ceiling; treat as targets to measure against, not a Meta-published standard.)

## Part V — ORAL LANGUAGE, AND THE STANDALONE-ORAL-TYPE VERDICT

The **Single National Curriculum for Urdu** organises the subject by competencies and — unlike the 2006 curriculum — **explicitly restores Listening & Speaking (سننا بولنا) as an assessed competency, alongside Reading, Writing, Vocabulary/Grammar, Speech, Creative Writing and "aesthetic sense," each with standards/benchmarks/SLOs and marking rubrics** ([SNC salient features](https://ncc.gov.pk/SiteImage/Downloads/Salient%20Features%20of%20SNC%20Primaryttt.pdf); [MoFEPT SNC](https://www.mofept.gov.pk/)). So oral language is *curricularly real* and the books are, as you note, oral-heavy. And the Urdu-medium evidence is that **richer oral-language input measurably lifts reading** — Urdu-medium children out-read English-medium peers on Urdu fluency, attributed to "increased oral language input" ([PMC — Predictors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303915/)).

**So should our taxonomy get an 8th, standalone oral/listening-speaking type? Decision: NO — fold it in. Here is the reasoning, and it is deliberate:**

1. **L1 ≠ L2 economics of attention.** In our Science-5 work, English is an *L2* — oral language is scarce and must be *protected* with its own time. Urdu here is the children's **mother tongue**: they already have abundant oral Urdu. The scarce, school-only skills are **decoding, fluency, and encoding (writing)**. An 8th "oral" slot would compete for the ~25–30 min against exactly the skills that are scarce — the opposite of what the ASER/EGRA gap demands.
2. **Oral is a *medium* woven through every type, not a competency to silo.** `buland_khwani` **is** oral language in the service of print (read-aloud, echo/choral, discussion of the passage). `tafheem` is listening/reading comprehension. `alfaaz_maani` is oral vocabulary. `takhleeqi_likhai` starts as oral composition ("say it, then write it"). Siloing "oral" would strip the oral life out of these and leave them mechanical.
3. **Where the books' oral richness lands.** Make it explicit that **`buland_khwani` carries speaking + read-aloud fluency**, and **`tafheem` carries listening comprehension** (teacher reads aloud → children answer). That covers the SNC سننا/بولنا competency *inside the existing 7* with no new type.

**Verdict, stated plainly:** keep 7 types; **do NOT add a standalone oral type**; assign the SNC oral competency to `buland_khwani` (speaking/read-aloud) + `tafheem` (listening). The one thing to add is a **required "oral-first" opening beat inside `takhleeqi_likhai` and `alfaaz_maani`** (say-before-write / say-before-read), so oral language is used as the on-ramp, not a separate destination.

## Part VI — WRITING (encoding is half of literacy, and the weaker half)

- **تختی / handwriting.** Legibility is a psychomotor skill needing explicit stroke instruction (stroke order, direction, baseline proportion, dot placement); an experimental Urdu study attributes poor handwriting to skipping structured تختی practice, and shows structured practice improves it ([PSSR — Improving Urdu Handwriting](https://pssr.org.pk/issues/v5/2/improving-urdu-hand-writing-an-experimental-study.pdf)). G1 must drill **letter formation in all positions on ruled lines** — this is the write-side twin of the RAN read-side drill.
- **املا / dictation = encoding, the mirror of decoding.** Dictation forces grapheme→phoneme mapping in reverse and is shown to build "letter recognition, word accuracy, and sentence writing" from the beginner level ([Imla foundational guide](https://ejournal.uiidalwa.ac.id/index.php/almuhawaroh/article/view/3503)). **Every phonics/joining lesson should end with a 2–3-min pointed dictation** of the day's letters/words — this is `takhleeqi_likhai`'s foundational (non-creative) tier and is currently the likeliest gap.
- **Creative-writing progression:** word → pointed sentence (G1–2) → sentence & short paragraph (G3) → guided paragraph with punctuation (G4) → multi-paragraph composition, self-editing (G5). Always **oral-rehearse before writing**.

## Part VII — DELIVERABLES

### A. G1–5 Urdu reading/writing scope & sequence (Qaida-to-fluency)

| Grade | Word-recognition (decode) | Fluency | Comprehension / oral | Writing (encode) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | Phonemic awareness (Urdu sounds, blend/segment); all letters + **4 positional forms**; dot-discrimination; aeraab (زبر/زیر/پیش/جزم/شد); joining متّصل/منفصل; blend CV/CVC — **fully pointed** | Read pointed 2–4-word strings; echo/choral | Listen to stories; oral vocab; answer literal Qs | تختی letter-formation (all positions); name + pointed-word املا |
| **G2** | Consolidate joining; multi-syllable pointed words; تنوین; high-frequency sight words | **Repeated reading** of pointed short paragraph; **~30–50 cwpm** | Retell a short pointed text; oral Qs | Pointed sentence املا; copy legibly |
| **G3** | **Begin fade of aeraab** (retain on new words); decode unfamiliar words | Story-level fluency; **~55–70 cwpm** | Retell gist; simple inference | Sentence-level free writing; paragraph املا |
| **G4** | Decode **near-unpointed** grade text; morphology (prefixes/root+affix) | **~70–85 cwpm**; expression/prosody | Inferential Qs; summarise | Guided paragraph; punctuation |
| **G5** | Fluent unpointed reading; vocabulary breadth | **≈90 cwpm** (proficient) | Summarise, evaluate, respond | Multi-paragraph composition; self-edit |

### B. What each of our 7 Urdu lesson TYPES must contain (mapped, with the fixes)

| Our type | Rope/SoR role | **Must contain to build real decoding + fluency** | Gap it usually has |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`arkaan_saazi`** (letter/sound building) | Phonemic awareness + phonics + RAN | **Print-free PA beat** (hear/blend/segment Urdu sounds) → grapheme intro with **all 4 positional forms** → **dot-discrimination** → aeraab → blend pointed syllables. Daily **5-min automaticity flash** (spaced). | Jumps to print with no ear-training; teaches one shape not four; unpointed too early |
| **`buland_khwani`** (oral reading fluency) **← also owns SPEAKING/oral** | Fluency + sight recognition + **oral language** | **Repeated reading** of a **pointed decodable** passage (3–4×); teacher-model → echo/choral → partner; discussion of the text = the speaking beat. Track cwpm. | Read-once, not re-read; text not decodable/pointed; no fluency measure |
| **`alfaaz_maani`** (vocabulary/meaning) | Vocabulary (language-comp) | **Oral-first**: use word in speech → meaning → then in print; tie new words to the passage; spaced review of prior words | Definitions in isolation, no oral on-ramp, no reuse |
| **`qawaid`** (grammar) | Language structures | Grammar **in the context of read/written sentences**, not rules in a vacuum; oral pattern-drill before written | Abstract rule memorisation |
| **`tafheem`** (comprehension) **← also owns LISTENING** | Language comprehension | **Both** listening comp (teacher reads → children answer) **and** reading comp; literal → inferential ladder by grade; retell/summarise | Only literal recall; no listening-comp; no inference progression |
| **`takhleeqi_likhai`** (creative writing) **← must add ENCODING tier** | Encoding + composition | **Foundational tier = تختی handwriting + 2–3-min pointed املا/dictation every lesson** (currently the biggest hole); then **oral-rehearse → write**; grade-stepped word→sentence→paragraph | Skips handwriting/dictation; "creative" with no encoding foundation |
| **`duhrai`** (revision) | Retrieval/spaced practice | **Spaced retrieval** of letters + joining + sight words + prior vocab (yesterday's + last-week's resurface); low-stakes, fast | Massed same-day review only; not spaced |

### C. Concrete drilling mechanics for a 25–30-min Urdu lesson

A fixed daily spine (mirrors how Science-5 fixes its 5E run):

1. **0–5 min — Automaticity warm-up (RAN engine, `arkaan_saazi`/`duhrai`).** Fast flash: positional-form naming + dot-discrimination + blend a few pointed syllables. **Spaced**: mix today's + prior items.
2. **5–8 min — Phonemic awareness / new grapheme (`arkaan_saazi`).** Ear first (hear/blend/segment the sound), then the letter in all four shapes + its aeraab.
3. **8–16 min — Fluency: repeated reading (`buland_khwani`).** Teacher models a **pointed decodable** passage → echo → choral → partner; 3–4 reads; brief text discussion (the **speaking** beat).
4. **16–22 min — Meaning (`tafheem` + `alfaaz_maani`).** Listening/reading comprehension Qs (literal→inferential by grade); pull 2–3 new words, used orally then in print.
5. **22–30 min — Encode (`takhleeqi_likhai`).** تختی handwriting (G1–2) + **2–3-min pointed املا/dictation** of the day's letters/words; oral-rehearse-then-write for composition (G3+).
   *`qawaid`* is threaded into steps 3–5 (grammar in real sentences), not given its own isolated block.

### D. Proficiency targets per grade — see Part IV table (ASER rung + cwpm band + encode target). Headline: **G1 letters+blend pointed / G2 ~30–50 cwpm pointed paragraph / G3 ~55–70 cwpm story, aeraab fading / G4 ~70–85 cwpm near-unpointed / G5 ≈90 cwpm unpointed + composition.**

### E. Gaps between best practice and a typical textbook lesson

1. **No print-free phonemic awareness.** Books start at letters; the *ear* work that best predicts Urdu reading (U-PASS) is absent. → `arkaan_saazi` must add it.
2. **Aeraab dropped too early / inconsistently.** Unpointed text in early grades manufactures guessers, the ASER failure mode. → enforce fully-pointed G1–2, staged fade.
3. **One shape taught, four needed.** Positional forms + dot-discrimination under-drilled — precisely the RAN/visual load that predicts Urdu reading. → daily automaticity flash.
4. **Read-once, not re-read; text isn't decodable.** No repeated-reading routine, no cwpm tracking, passages not restricted to taught letters. → `buland_khwani` = repeated reading of decodable pointed text + fluency measure.
5. **Encoding is the missing half.** تختی handwriting + املا/dictation are assumed, not scheduled. → make dictation a *required closing beat* of every lesson.
6. **Comprehension stuck at literal recall; no listening-comp.** → `tafheem` owns a literal→inferential ladder and teacher-read-aloud listening comp.
7. **Revision is massed, not spaced.** → `duhrai` = spaced retrieval with prior items resurfacing.
8. **The taxonomy has no *risk* of an oral gap — the risk is the opposite** (over-weighting oral, which the child already has, at the expense of decoding/writing time). Confirmed: **no 8th type.**
